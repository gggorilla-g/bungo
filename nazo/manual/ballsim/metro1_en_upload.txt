【タイトル】
100 Glass Metronomes. Release the Board. Will They All Sync? #shorts #satisfying #physics

【説明】
100 glass metronomes stand on a platform. 98 of them tick at 120 BPM. The two red ones are built a little fast, at 120.5 and 121. At first the board is locked with wedges and they tick at random. After 6 seconds the board is released. Will all 100 sync?

Answer: yes. Even the two with a different speed get pulled to the same rate.

How it works:
- The platform sits on two rollers. Once the wedges are removed it is free to slide sideways, like a base-isolated building.
- While the board is locked, nothing connects the metronomes, so they stay scattered forever.
- When the metronomes swing, the platform moves a little the other way. Through that motion the metronomes pull on each other. The red mark below shows where the platform is.
- In this video each one is pulled slightly toward the average swing of the group (the Kuramoto model). The more of them are in sync, the stronger the pull.
- If the speed difference is small, the pull wins and the rates become equal. Those two settle just barely ahead of the others (up to about 3 degrees of needle angle).
- A metronome that is far off (say 140 BPM) would never sync with this much pull.
- The color shows how far each one is from the group. As the gap shrinks, the colors drift toward the same light blue.
- The sound is a glass note every time a needle passes the center.

Simulation, sound and animation: original (Python).
