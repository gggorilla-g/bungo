【タイトル】
98 Glass Metronomes and 2 Rebels. Will All 100 Sync? #shorts #satisfying #physics

【説明】
100 glass metronomes stand on a platform. 98 of them tick at 120 BPM. Rebel A ticks at 122 and rebel B at 125. They all start at random. Will all 100 ever sync?

Answer: yes. First the 98 fall into step, then A is pulled in, and finally B.

How it works:
- The platform sits on two rollers, so it is free to slide sideways, like a base-isolated building.
- When the metronomes swing, the platform moves a little the other way. Through that motion the metronomes pull on each other. The red mark below shows where the platform is.
- In this video each one is pulled slightly toward the average swing of the group (the Kuramoto model). The more of them are in sync, the stronger the pull.
- A is only a little faster, so it gets pulled in early. B is further off and cannot sync until the pull of the group has grown strong.
- Here "in sync" means ticking at the same rate. B ends up at the same rate but swings a little behind the others.
- A metronome that is much further off (say 140 BPM) would never sync with this much pull.
- The color shows how far each one is from the group. As the gap shrinks, the colors drift toward the same light blue.
- The sound is a glass note every time a needle passes the center.

Simulation, sound and animation: original (Python).
