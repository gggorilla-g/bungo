【タイトル】
Each Bounce Paints One Piece of the Ring. Can It Paint All of It? Guess the Tune #shorts #satisfying #physics

【説明】
A ball flies in straight lines inside a circle. Every bounce paints one piece of the ring where it hit, and plays the next note of a tune. Will the whole ring get painted? Can you name the tune?

Answer: Pachelbel's Canon (written around 1680, public domain).

How it works:
- Inside a circle, a ball travels the same distance between every two bounces. So the hits come at a steady beat.
- Each bounce lands three eighths of a turn further on. After 8 bounces, all 8 pieces of the ring are painted.
- Every time a ring is full, the balls double (1, 2, 4, 8). The new balls follow the same shape of path, half a beat and half a piece apart.
- So the notes go from quarter notes to 8th, 16th and 32nd notes, and the pieces go 8, 16, 32, 64. That is 120 bounces in total.
- The thin lines are the paths. They pile up and a small circle appears in the middle, because every path passes the center at the same distance.
- Balls pass through each other.

- Every hit is a small hard ball tapping a wooden plate.

Simulation, sound and animation: original (Python).
