【タイトル】
One Ball Hits Two at Once. How Far Does It Spread? #shorts #physics #math

【説明】
A ball hits two identical resting balls at the same moment, one at 45 degrees on each side. The incoming ball stops dead, and the two fly off at a right angle to each other. Each of them then hits its own pair, and so on: 1, 2, 4 ... 256 balls, and their trails grow into a branching tree.

How it works:
- In a perfectly elastic collision between equal masses, this symmetric hit stops the incoming ball, and each target leaves at about 71% (1/sqrt(2)) of its speed. Momentum and energy are both the same before and after.
- The distance to the next pair is also shortened to about 71% each time. Since the speed drops by the same factor, every generation takes the same time.
- This is a thought experiment: no air or friction, and each ball only hits its own pair.
- Every collision plays one note. Later generations play higher notes.

Simulation, sound and animation: original (Python).
