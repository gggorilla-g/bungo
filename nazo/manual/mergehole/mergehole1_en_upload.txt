【タイトル】
Same Stars Merge Into a Heavier One. How Many Suns Make a Black Hole? #Shorts #space #blackhole

【説明】
Suns drop one at a time. When two stars of the same mass touch, they merge into one heavier star (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32).
The 64th Sun turns the pile into a black hole. After that it swallows every Sun that falls in, and it keeps getting heavier and bigger.

- Merging by doubling is a rule of this video only
- The masses follow real stars: Sirius is about 2 Suns, Regulus about 4, Bellatrix about 8, Betelgeuse about 10 to 20, Alnitak about 33
- Stars heavier than about 20 Suns are thought to be able to end their lives as black holes (where exactly the line is, is still uncertain)
- A black hole's radius is proportional to its mass (the Schwarzschild radius). At 512 Suns it is 8 times the radius at 64 Suns
- The black hole is drawn by computing how light bends around it (a non-rotating black hole with a disk of gas, seen almost edge-on). The side moving toward you looks brighter, the side moving away looks dimmer and redder. Colors and brightness are adjusted to be easy to see
- The warping of the stars and the tube behind it uses a simplified formula for bent light
- Each sound plays at the moment stars touch or get swallowed. Bigger stars sound lower
