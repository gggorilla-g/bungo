【タイトル】
Bigger Bubbles Make Lower Sounds. Popping Bubbles Play the Canon Chords, Then the Whale Plays One #Shorts #whale #relaxing

【説明】
Bubbles rise from the sea floor and ring the moment they pop at the surface. Watch them reach the surface line like notes in a rhythm game.

- Bigger bubbles make lower sounds (the pitch of a bubble ringing in water is inversely proportional to its radius: the Minnaert resonance). Here, a bubble one octave higher has half the radius
- Bigger bubbles rise faster (speed is taken as proportional to the square root of the radius). Smaller bubbles leave earlier so that every bubble reaches the surface on the beat
- The music is two rounds of the Canon chord progression (D A Bm F#m G D G A). In round two, the golden bubbles carry the melody
- At the end the whale blows eight bubbles at once. The big ones reach the surface first, so the chord rolls from low to high
- A sound plays only when a bubble pops at the surface (a glass tone plus the short ring of the bubble itself). 119 bubbles in total
- The link between bubble size and pitch follows real bubbles, but sizes, speeds and timbre are adjusted to be easy to see and hear

Simulation, sound and animation: original (made in Python)
