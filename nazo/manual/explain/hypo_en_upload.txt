How Romans Heated Their Floors 2,000 Years Ago #shorts

【説明】
Romans heated floors and walls with fire and hot air: the hypocaust.

The facts:
- A raised floor stood on short stacks of bricks (pilae), leaving a hollow space underneath. In the Baths of Caracalla, that cavity was about 2 feet (about 60 cm) deep.
- A wood-fired furnace (praefurnium), usually outside the heated room, sent hot air and smoke into the space under the floor.
- Hot air rose through hollow clay flues inside the walls (tubuli) and left near the roof, so the walls were heated too.
- Keeping the fires going took a lot of labor: workers fed in small wood and cleared out ash and soot.
- The animation is simplified.
Sources: Vitruvius, De architectura (via Wikipedia, "Praefurnium"); Engineering Rome, "Thermal Respite in Ancient Rome: A Study of the Baths of Caracalla".
