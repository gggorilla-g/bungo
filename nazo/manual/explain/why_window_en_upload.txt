Why Airplane Windows Have a Tiny Hole #shorts

【説明】
Why does every airplane window have a tiny hole? It has two jobs.

The facts:
- Most airliner windows are made of three acrylic panes: an inner scratch pane, a middle pane, and an outer pane.
- At cruising altitude the outside air pressure is roughly a quarter of sea level, while the cabin is pressurized, so the cabin air pushes outward on the window.
- A tiny "breather" (bleed) hole in the middle pane lets the pressure between the panes match the cabin, so the outer pane carries the load. The middle pane is a fail-safe if the outer pane fails.
- The hole also lets air move in the gap, which helps keep the window from fogging up or frosting.
Sources: Popular Mechanics / Travel + Leisure explainers on airplane window breather holes; International Standard Atmosphere (about 24% of sea-level pressure at 35,000 ft).
